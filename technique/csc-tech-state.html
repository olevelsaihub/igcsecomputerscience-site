Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Computer Science · 4CP0

State, Give, Identify, Name (1-2 marks)

Recall a fact, term, example or short definition, or pick out the key information from a figure, table or piece of code. No reasoning is needed.

Command words: State, Give, Identify, NameUsually 1-2 marksAbout 1 minute per mark.

What Pearson asks

Recall a fact, term, example or short definition, or pick out the key information from a figure, table or piece of code. No reasoning is needed.

How the marks are earned

Each correct, distinct point earns one mark. A two-mark 'give two' question needs two different ideas; saying the same thing twice in other words only scores once. Answers that just repeat words already in the question, or are too vague, are not credited. Correct technical terms (cache, IMAP, indentation) score better than everyday words.

Method

  1. Count the marks and the number asked for (one, two).

    Why: Each mark usually needs its own separate point.

  2. Use the precise computing term, then add a few words if the term alone could be unclear.

    Why: Specific vocabulary is what the scheme looks for.

  3. Check that your answer is not already given in the question.

    Why: Repeating the stem earns nothing.

  4. For 'give two', make the two points about different things.

    Why: Two versions of one idea count as one mark.

  5. Keep it short and move on.

    Why: Extra explanation earns no extra marks and costs time for longer questions.

Answer frame

1. [precise term or fact] 2. [a different term or fact]

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Giving two answers that are really the same point in different words.
  • Using everyday language (such as 'makes it safe') instead of the computing term.
  • Repeating information that is already printed in the question.
  • Writing a long explanation and running out of time later.
  • Giving more answers than asked; usually only the first ones are marked.

So that if the data is intercepted or stolen, it cannot be understood by anyone who does not have the key.

Why it earns the marks

It gives one clear reason in a single sentence: encryption keeps the meaning hidden from people who are not allowed to read it. It does not waste time describing how a cipher works, which the question did not ask for.
