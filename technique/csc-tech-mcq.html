Multiple choice (1 mark)

Choose the single correct option, usually from four labelled A to D, and mark it by putting a cross in its box. The stem often starts with Identify, so you are picking one key fact out of a list.

Command word: multiple-choiceUsually 1 markAbout 1 minute; move on quickly if stuck and come back.

What Pearson asks

Choose the single correct option, usually from four labelled A to D, and mark it by putting a cross in its box. The stem often starts with Identify, so you are picking one key fact out of a list.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the one correct box. Nothing is given for working, and marking two boxes earns nothing, so a clear single choice is what counts. The wrong options are usually close cousins of the right one, such as a related component or the result of a common slip in a calculation.

Method

  1. Read the stem and cover the options. Say or jot your own answer first.

    Why: Working it out before you look stops a plausible-looking distractor from pulling you away.

  2. For a number or binary question, do the working in the margin.

    Why: Distractors are often the answer you get from a typical mistake, so a quick check catches it.

  3. Find the option that matches your answer. If none matches, cross out the ones you know are wrong.

    Why: Eliminating two options turns a guess into a 50-50 choice.

  4. Watch for key words such as NOT, most, best and first.

    Why: One small word can flip which option is correct.

  5. Put one clear cross in one box. To change it, put a line through the box and cross the new one.

    Why: An unclear or double mark cannot be credited.

Answer frame

My own answer first: ... -> matches option ... -> cross that box only.

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Choosing the first option that looks familiar before reading all four.
  • Picking a distractor that comes from a common calculation slip, such as a logical shift instead of an arithmetic one.
  • Missing a negative word such as NOT in the stem.
  • Marking two boxes, or changing an answer without making the final choice clear.
  • Leaving the question blank; a blank can never score, so always choose.

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

This part asks for one exact binary answer, which is how many multiple-choice items work. Before looking at any options, shift 1100 0101 right one place at a time, copying the left-hand bit back in each time because it is an arithmetic shift: 1110 0010, then 1111 0001, then 1111 1000. Answer: 1111 1000. If options were given, 0001 1000 would be the trap, because that is what a logical shift (filling with zeros) produces.

Why it earns the marks

The single correct pattern earns the mark. Working it out first, step by step in the margin, means the likely distractor (the logical-shift result) is spotted and rejected instead of chosen.
