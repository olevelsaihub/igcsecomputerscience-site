Knowledge card

Technology, the environment and health

Written

Technology affects the environment at every stage: making, running and throwing away. Wearables can help health. Give specific impacts and actions, then the result.

  • Making computers uses large amounts of energy and water and causes air and water pollution.
  • Devices need metals such as copper, gold and lithium; mining them uses up limited resources and can poison soil and groundwater.
  • Running computers and data centres uses a lot of electricity, including for cooling.
  • E-waste (thrown-away electronics) contains toxic materials; in landfill these can leak into the ground and water.
  • Ways to reduce the impact: switch devices off when not in use, lower screen brightness, keep devices longer, repair them, recycle them properly, or donate them.
  • Keeping a device longer means fewer new devices are made, so less manufacturing and less e-waste.
  • Wearable technology such as smartwatches can benefit health by measuring heart rate, steps, sleep and blood oxygen, and by encouraging exercise.
  • Wearables can share data with phone apps and doctors, and can send alerts, but they only help if the user acts on the information.

The method

  1. Pick the stage

    Making, using, throwing away, or health.

  2. Be specific

    A named action or impact.

  3. Explain

    Action, so environmental benefit.

  4. Discuss

    Several stages, developed, plus a balance.

  5. Answer the part

    Impacts and actions both, if asked.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Vague actions

    Name the action.

  • ✗
    Impacts with no 'why'

    Say why each matters.

  • ✗
    Wrong side

    Benefits or harm: read which.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Give1One specific action, such as 'lower the screen brightness'.
Explain1Action + environmental effect, for example 'keep the phone for longer, so fewer new phones are manufactured and less e-waste is created'.
Discuss2Levels-marked out of 6, so there is no list of ticks. Write 3-4 short paragraphs, one for each bullet or idea, and in each one make a point, then say why it matters or what it leads to. (c) Wearable technology, such as smartwatches, has become very popular with many groups of people including athletes. Discuss how the use of wearable technology can be a benefit to health. (6)

Worked explanation

  1. Plan three ideas: what wearables measure, how the data is used, and how they change behaviour.
  2. Develop each idea: say what the device does, then how that helps health.
  3. Add a balancing point: the device only helps if the person uses the information.
  4. Finish with a short conclusion.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Wearable devices such as smartwatches contain sensors that measure things like heart rate, blood oxygen and sleep. This gives people a clear picture of their health each day, so they can notice problems such as an unusually high resting heart rate and get help early.

Many wearables use GPS and motion sensors to record steps, distance and calories used. Athletes can use this to plan training and see their progress, which helps them improve safely without overtraining.

The watch can send its data over a Bluetooth link to a phone app, which analyses trends over weeks and can share them with a coach or doctor. It can also vibrate to remind someone who has been sitting still for too long, and it can alert family if an elderly person falls, helping them live independently.

Goals, badges and praise for reaching targets motivate many people to keep exercising. However, the benefit depends on the user: some people stop wearing the device or ignore its advice, so it does not automatically make anyone healthier.

Overall, wearables can support better health by measuring, analysing and motivating, as long as the wearer acts on the information.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked. This answer covers several distinct benefits (sensor readings, activity tracking, linked apps and alerts, motivation), develops each with its effect on health, and adds a balancing point and conclusion, which fits the top level (5-6 marks).

Grade 9 top-mark questions

The highest-mark questions of this type, with an answer plan for each. Full model answers are written by olevels.com and shared by your teacher after you have attempted the question.

June 2021 · Paper 1 · Q6(c)

Answer plan

  1. Spot the two things asked for: negative environmental impacts AND actions to reduce them.
  2. Organise impacts by life cycle: making (mining, water, energy, pollution), using (electricity, cooling), disposing (toxic e-waste, landfill).
  3. For each impact, explain why it harms the environment, not just name it.
  4. Give 3-4 actions and link each one to the impact it tackles (switch off saves energy; recycling reduces mining; donating extends life).
  5. Mention who can act: individuals, manufacturers or sellers, governments.
  6. Conclude: the impact cannot be removed but can be reduced, saying which actions matter most.

June 2025 · Paper 1 · Q5(c)

Answer plan

  1. Note the focus: benefits to health of wearables, and the groups named (athletes and others).
  2. Point 1: sensors measure heart rate, steps, calories, sleep, blood oxygen continuously; why continuous data is useful.
  3. Point 2: GPS and linked apps (Bluetooth or internet) analyse trends; how athletes use this to train better.
  4. Point 3: reminders, goals and rewards motivate ordinary users to move more.
  5. Point 4: alerts such as irregular heartbeat or fall detection help vulnerable people live independently.
  6. Balance: accuracy limits, people stop using them, privacy of health data.
  7. Conclude: real benefits, but only if the wearer acts on the data.
