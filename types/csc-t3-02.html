Knowledge card

Sign and magnitude numbers

Written
  • A signed integer is a whole number that can be positive or negative.
  • In sign and magnitude, the leftmost bit (the most significant bit, MSB) is the sign bit.
  • A sign bit of 0 means positive; a sign bit of 1 means negative.
  • The other 7 bits hold the magnitude, which is the size of the number without its sign.
  • The 7 magnitude bits have place values 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1.
  • In 8 bits the range is -127 (1111 1111) to +127 (0111 1111).
  • Sign and magnitude has two patterns for zero: 0000 0000 and 1000 0000.
  • To write a negative number, convert its size to 7 bits and put a 1 in front.

Before you begin

  • Unsigned binary to denary conversion (CSC-T3-01)
  • Place values 64 down to 1
1

The method

  1. Separate the leftmost bit from the other seven bits.

    Why: The first bit only tells you the sign; it has no place value in this system.

  2. Pattern to denary: read the sign from the first bit (1 is minus, 0 is plus).

    Why: One mark usually depends on getting the sign right.

  3. Convert the remaining seven bits using 64 32 16 8 4 2 1 and add them.

    Why: This gives the size of the number.

  4. Denary to pattern: convert the size (ignore the minus sign) to 7 bits, then put 1 in front for negative or 0 for positive.

    Why: This builds the sign bit and the magnitude separately, just as the system stores them.

  5. For the range, set all seven magnitude bits to 1 (127) and use sign bit 1 for the lowest and 0 for the highest.

    Why: The largest size the seven bits can hold is 127.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Leaving out the minus sign in a denary answer, which loses the sign mark.
  • ✗
    Counting the sign bit as 128 and adding it to the total.
  • ✗
    Using two's complement (flip and add 1) when the question says sign and magnitude.
  • ✗
    Giving -128 as the lowest value; that is the two's complement limit, not sign and magnitude.
  • ✗
    Writing only 7 bits and forgetting the sign bit.

What a complete response includes

Two marks split into the sign and the size: one mark for the sign (the minus symbol, or an MSB of 1), and one for the correct magnitude or remaining seven bits. Range tables earn one mark per fully correct row.

Worked explanation

  1. The number is negative, so the sign bit is 1.
  2. Now convert the size, 43, into 7 bits using 64 32 16 8 4 2 1.
  3. 64 does not fit: 0. 32 fits: 1, leaving 11. 16 does not fit: 0. 8 fits: 1, leaving 3.
  4. 4 does not fit: 0. 2 fits: 1, leaving 1. 1 fits: 1, leaving 0.
  5. Magnitude bits: 010 1011.
  6. Put the sign bit in front: 1010 1011.
  7. Check: sign 1 means minus; 32 + 8 + 2 + 1 = 43, so the pattern is -43.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1010 1011

How the marks are earned

One mark for the leftmost bit being 1 (the sign), and one mark for the other seven bits 010 1011 being correct.

Calculation check

  • '1' + format(43, '07b') = 10101011
  • int('0101011', 2) = 43
  • -(32 + 8 + 2 + 1) = -43
