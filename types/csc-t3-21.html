Knowledge card

Pigpen cipher

Written
  • The Pigpen cipher is a substitution cipher: each letter is replaced by a symbol.
  • The key is a set of grids: usually two noughts-and-crosses grids and two X shapes, one of each with dots.
  • A letter's symbol is the shape of the lines around its cell, plus a dot if the letter is in a dotted grid.
  • In the usual layout, A to I are in the plain grid, J to R in the dotted grid, S to V in the plain X and W to Z in the dotted X.
  • To decode, match each symbol's shape and dot to a cell in the grid and read its letter.
  • A valid Pigpen key gives every letter a symbol, and no two letters share the same symbol.

Before you begin

  • Purpose of encryption (CSC-T3-17)
  • Substitution versus transposition

The method

  1. Find the letter in the grids and note which grid it is in (plain or dotted, square grid or X).

    Why: The grid decides whether a dot is needed and what kind of lines surround it.

  2. Draw only the lines that border that letter's cell.

    Why: The outline of the cell is the symbol.

  3. Add a dot inside the shape if the letter is in a dotted grid.

    Why: The dot separates letters that share the same outline.

  4. To decode, reverse this: match the outline and the dot to a cell.

    Why: Each symbol points to exactly one cell.

  5. To judge a grid, check every letter has a symbol and no symbol is used twice.

    Why: These are the two conditions for a working substitution key.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Forgetting the dot for letters in a dotted grid.
  • ✗
    Drawing the whole square instead of only the lines that border the cell.
  • ✗
    Mixing up left and right, for example drawing the mirror image of the symbol.
  • ✗
    Calling Pigpen a shift cipher.

Worked explanation

  1. Figure 1 uses the usual layout: A to I in the plain grid, J to R in the dotted grid, S to V in the plain X and W to Z in the dotted X.
  2. M is in the dotted grid, middle row, left cell (J K L / M N O / P Q R).
  3. That cell has a line above, a line below and a line on its right, so its symbol is a box open on the left (like ]) with a dot inside.
  4. A is in the plain grid, top-left cell. It has a line on its right and a line below, so its symbol is a corner shape like _| with no dot.
  5. Y is in the dotted X, right-hand section. Its two edges meet at the centre to its left, so its symbol is a < shape with a dot inside.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

M: a box open on the left (three sides: top, right, bottom) with a dot inside. A: a corner made of a right-hand line and a bottom line (_|), no dot. Y: a < shape (open to the right) with a dot inside.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct symbol: M, A and Y.

Calculation check

  • 'JKLMNOPQR'.index('M') = 3
  • divmod('ABCDEFGHI'.index('A'), 3) = (0, 0)
  • 'WXYZ'.index('Y') = 2
