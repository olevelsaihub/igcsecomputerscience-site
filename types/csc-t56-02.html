Knowledge card

Wired or wireless? Media, pros and cons, and network speed units

Written
  • Wired networks send data along cables: copper twisted-pair cable carries electrical signals, and fibre-optic cable carries pulses of light.
  • Wireless networks send data through the air using radio waves; Wi-Fi uses the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz frequency bands.
  • Advantages of wired: a steadier, more reliable connection that does not weaken with distance from the router or through walls, and it is harder for outsiders to intercept.
  • Wired links often give higher bandwidth (more bits per second) than wireless links.
  • Disadvantages of wired: cables must be installed and maintained, devices are tied to one place by the cable length, loose cables can be a trip hazard, and some devices such as phones have no network port.
  • Network data speeds are measured in bits per second, for example megabits per second (Mbps) and gigabits per second (Gbps).
  • Bytes and mebibits or mebibytes are units of storage, not network speed.
  • A personal hotspot can be better than free public Wi-Fi because fewer people share the bandwidth and only devices you allow can join, so it is more secure.

The method

  1. Check which way round the comparison goes, for example advantages of wired rather than wireless.

    Why: An advantage of one is a disadvantage of the other, so reading the direction wrongly loses every mark.

  2. Pick features from this list: reliability, effect of distance and walls, bandwidth, security, mobility, installation.

    Why: These are the features examiners credit; each gives one separate point.

  3. State each point as a comparison, for example 'more secure because the signal cannot be picked up from outside'.

    Why: A comparison shows which medium is better on that feature.

  4. For Explain or Describe, add the consequence or the other side with 'so' or 'whereas'.

    Why: The second mark is for the linked consequence or the contrasting side.

  5. For speed units, choose bits per second with a mega or giga prefix.

    Why: Speeds are always in bits, never bytes or mebi units.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying wireless travels by 'waves' only, without saying radio waves or through the air.
  • ✗
    Choosing megabytes per second or mebibits per second as the unit of network speed.
  • ✗
    Giving the same feature twice, such as 'more secure' and 'harder to hack'.
  • ✗
    Answering the opposite direction (giving advantages of wireless when asked for advantages of wired).
  • ✗
    Saying only 'electricity' for copper cables instead of electrical signals.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice3Choose exactly one letter. Cross out a wrong choice cleanly; two marked boxes score zero. For speed units, the answer is always a bits-per-second unit with a decimal prefix.
State2Two short, different points, each written as a comparison with the other medium.
Explain1One point plus its reason, joined with 'because' or 'so', tied to the scenario.
Describe1Give both sides: what the wired network uses, whereas what the wireless network uses.

What a complete response includes

One mark per valid, separate advantage or disadvantage. Describe and Explain items give the second mark for the contrasting side or the consequence. Multiple-choice is one mark.

Worked explanation

  1. Decide the feature: the physical medium the data travels through.
  2. Wired side: cables, either copper (electrical signals) or fibre-optic (light).
  3. Wireless side: through the air as radio waves.
  4. Join both sides with 'whereas' so it reads as one difference.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

In a wired network the data packets travel along cables, as electrical signals in copper cable or as pulses of light in fibre-optic cable, whereas in a wireless network the packets travel through the air as radio waves.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the wired medium (copper or fibre-optic cable). One mark for the wireless medium (radio waves through the air).
