Knowledge card

Cyber attacks: phishing, shoulder surfing, eavesdropping and unpatched software

Written

Name the attack, say what the attacker exploits, then what they want. Phishing and shoulder surfing trick people; eavesdropping intercepts data in transit.

  • Social engineering means tricking people, rather than breaking technology, into giving away information or access.
  • Phishing is a social engineering attack: a fake email or message that pretends to come from a trusted organisation to get you to click a link or give personal details.
  • Signs of phishing include a generic greeting such as 'Dear Customer', a sender address that does not match the real company, urgent threats, spelling mistakes and a link to log in.
  • Pharming secretly redirects a user from a genuine website to a fake one that collects their details.
  • Shoulder surfing is watching someone, directly or with a camera, to see their password, PIN or private information as they type or read it.
  • Ways to stop shoulder surfing: cover the keypad, use a privacy screen filter, sit with your back to a wall, and avoid entering private details in public.
  • Eavesdropping is intercepting data while it travels across a network.
  • Unpatched software has security weaknesses that are publicly known, so attackers can use them until the patch (update) is installed.
  • Infected USB devices and other digital devices can bring malware into a network.

The method

  1. Name the attack

    From the description.

  2. What is exploited?

    A person's trust, or a technical weakness.

  3. What do they want?

    Passwords, PINs, details, control.

  4. Prevention

    A measure and why it blocks that attack.

  5. Phishing clues

    Circle concrete clues.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Example not definition

    Say what the attack is.

  • ✗
    Mixing the attacks

    Eavesdropping is data; shoulder surfing is a person.

  • ✗
    Measure, no reason

    Say why it works.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Give1One short reason or clue, linked to the text shown.
Identify1Circle or point to two separate clues in the given email, such as the odd sender address and the log-in link.
Describe1What the attacker does + what they are trying to get.
Explain2Measure or weakness + its effect, joined with 'so' or 'because'.
State1One clear sentence that defines the term; an example alone is not enough.
multiple-choice1Choose exactly one letter. Cross out a wrong choice cleanly; two marked boxes score zero. Watch the near-miss options: phishing, shoulder surfing and pharming are all offered as distractors.

Worked explanation

  1. Action: someone watches the user while they use a device.
  2. Aim: to see and steal a password, PIN or other private details.
  3. Put both parts in one sentence and add an everyday example.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Shoulder surfing is when another person secretly watches someone using a laptop, phone or keypad, for example by looking over their shoulder in a café, so that they can see and steal their password, PIN or other private information.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the action (a third party watches or spies on the user). One mark for the aim (to obtain their password, PIN or sensitive details).
