Knowledge card

What encryption is and why we use it

Written
  • Plaintext is a readable message before it is encrypted.
  • Ciphertext is the scrambled, unreadable message after encryption.
  • Encryption turns plaintext into ciphertext using an algorithm and a key.
  • Decryption turns ciphertext back into plaintext using the key.
  • Encryption is used so that only the intended receiver, who has the key, can understand the data.
  • Encryption does not stop data being intercepted or stolen; it stops thieves understanding it.
  • Encryption does not stop hackers getting into a network and does not make files smaller.

Before you begin

  • Basic idea of a network and data being sent
1

The method

  1. For a definition, say what goes in and what comes out: plaintext is turned into ciphertext.

    Why: That is the meaning examiners look for.

  2. For a reason, say who can and cannot understand the data: only authorised people with the key.

    Why: A reason must mention understanding or reading, not just 'security'.

  3. Avoid saying it stops hacking or access.

    Why: Encryption protects meaning, not entry.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'to stop hackers' or 'to stop access', which is not what encryption does.
  • ✗
    Swapping plaintext and ciphertext.
  • ✗
    Confusing encryption with compression.
  • ✗
    Choosing the decryption option in a multiple-choice question.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State2One short sentence: for example, converting plaintext into unreadable ciphertext, or so unauthorised people cannot read the card details.
Give1One reason about only authorised people being able to read the data.
multiple-choice2Pick the option about plaintext to ciphertext, or about only the intended receiver understanding the data.

Worked explanation

  1. Think about what happens if someone intercepts the data.
  2. Encrypted data looks like nonsense without the key.
  3. So the reason is about who can understand it, not about stopping access.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

So that if the data is intercepted it cannot be understood; only an authorised person with the key can read it.

How the marks are earned

One mark for linking encryption to only authorised people being able to read or understand the data.
