Knowledge card

Emerging tech: quantum, DNA computing, AI and nanotechnology

Written

Know each technology's key idea: quantum uses qubits and superposition, DNA computing uses DNA and chemistry, AI learns from data, nanotechnology works at atomic scale.

  • Classical computers store data as bits, each 0 or 1.
  • Quantum computers store data in qubits (quantum bits), made from sub-atomic particles such as electrons or photons.
  • Superposition means a qubit can be in a combination of 0 and 1 at the same time; its value is only fixed when it is measured.
  • Entanglement links pairs of qubits so that the state of one affects the other; together with superposition this lets quantum computers explore many possibilities at once.
  • DNA computing uses strands of DNA and chemical reactions, instead of silicon chips and electricity, and can run huge numbers of operations in parallel.
  • DNA uses a four-letter code (A, C, G, T) rather than the two binary digits 0 and 1.
  • Artificial intelligence (AI) is software that learns patterns from large sets of training data and uses them to make predictions or decisions about new data.
  • AI is used in medical diagnosis, chatbots, face and speech recognition, recommendations, game playing and self-driving vehicles.
  • AI raises ethical issues: biased training data can give unfair results, it can be hard to know if its answer is right, and some jobs may be replaced.
  • Nanotechnology is engineering at the scale of atoms and molecules, roughly 1 to 100 nanometres; uses include tiny transistors, scratch-resistant coatings and medicines delivered to exact places in the body.

Worked explanation

  1. Notice that the stem already gives superposition, so it cannot be used.
  2. Characteristic 1: data is held in qubits made from sub-atomic particles.
  3. Characteristic 2: qubits can be entangled, so one affects the other.
  4. Write each on its own line with a short explanation.
  5. Other characteristics also accepted as answers: a quantum computer can work on many possible values at the same time, so some problems (such as searching or code-breaking) are solved far faster; qubits must be kept extremely cold and isolated, so the machines are large and expensive.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. Quantum computers store data in qubits (quantum bits), which are made from sub-atomic particles such as electrons or photons. 2. Pairs of qubits can be entangled, meaning the state of one qubit is linked to and affects the state of the other.

How the marks are earned

One mark for qubits or sub-atomic particles. One mark for entanglement.

June 2019 · Paper 1 · Q6(c)

Answer plan

  1. Underline the three areas you must cover: characteristics, uses, ethical issues.
  2. Characteristics (1 short paragraph): trained on example data, finds patterns, predicts for new data, can improve over time.
  3. Uses (2 developed points): pick familiar areas such as recommendations, face or speech recognition, medical diagnosis; for each say what the AI does and why it helps.
  4. Ethical issues (2-3 developed points): biased training data, decisions nobody can explain or check, privacy of collected data, job losses; link each back to how AI learns.
  5. Use connectives (because, so, this means, however) so every point leads to a consequence.
  6. Conclude: agree or disagree with the statement and name what would make the impact positive (checked data, human oversight, laws).
