Knowledge card

How hard drives, optical discs and SSDs store data

Written

Name the medium, say how it is read, then say how 1s and 0s are shown. Magnetic uses polarity, optical uses pits and lands, solid state uses trapped charge.

  • Secondary storage keeps data permanently, even when the power is off.
  • A magnetic hard disc drive (HDD) stores data on spinning discs called platters, divided into tracks and sectors.
  • In an HDD, a read/write head on a moving arm reads and changes tiny magnetised areas; one magnetic direction (polarity) is 1, the other is 0.
  • Optical discs (CD, DVD) store data as pits and lands on a spiral track, read by a laser.
  • Light reflects differently from pits and lands, and the changes in reflection are read as 1s and 0s.
  • A solid state drive (SSD) uses flash memory chips with no moving parts.
  • In an SSD, transistors trap electrical charge; whether a cell holds charge represents a 0 or a 1, and the charge stays without power.
  • Compared with an HDD, an SSD is faster, quieter, lighter, uses less power and is tougher because it has no moving parts.

The method

  1. Pick the technology

    Magnetic, optical or solid state.

  2. Name the medium

    Platter, disc surface or flash chip.

  3. Say how it is read

    Moving head, laser or trapped charge.

  4. Link to binary

    Polarity, reflection, or charge.

  5. Choice question

    Give a property and link it to the device.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Mixing types

    A hard drive never uses a laser.

  • ✗
    Head on an SSD

    An SSD has no read/write head.

  • ✗
    Just saying better

    Say why: for example no moving parts.

Worked explanation

  1. Identify the technology: magnetic hard drive.
  2. Name the medium: data is on a spinning platter, split into tracks and sectors.
  3. Say how it is read: the arm moves the read/write head over the right track.
  4. Say how binary is shown: the head senses the magnetic polarity of each tiny area; one polarity means 1 and the other means 0.
  5. Write three clear, separate points.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The data is stored on a spinning platter that is divided into tracks and sectors. An arm moves the read/write head to the required track. The head detects the polarity of each magnetised area, where one polarity represents 1 and the opposite polarity represents 0.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for: the platter divided into tracks and sectors; the arm moving the head to the track; the head reading the values with polarity standing for 1 and 0 (up to three).
