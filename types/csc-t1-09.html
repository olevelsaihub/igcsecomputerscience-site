Knowledge card

Merge sort: split, then merge

Written

Merge sort splits the list in half again and again until every list has one item, then merges neighbouring sorted lists, always taking the smaller front item first.

  • Merge sort is a divide and conquer algorithm: it splits a problem into smaller parts, solves them, then combines the results.
  • The list is split in half again and again until every list holds just one item.
  • A list with one item is already sorted.
  • Merging takes two sorted lists and builds one sorted list by repeatedly taking the smaller (or larger, for descending) front item.
  • Merging goes in stages: single items into pairs, pairs into fours, and so on until one list remains.
  • Merge sort needs extra memory for the new lists it creates, unlike bubble sort, which sorts in place.

The method

  1. Split in halves

    Keep halving each list.

  2. Reach singles

    Every list has one item.

  3. Merge to pairs

    Neighbours only.

  4. Merge to fours

    Then the full list.

  5. Check direction

    Ascending or descending.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Sorting early

    Only split first; sort when merging.

  • ✗
    Not neighbours

    Merge side-by-side lists.

  • ✗
    Skipping a level

    Pairs, then fours, then all.

Worked explanation

  1. Start: 9, 1, 7, 6, 3, 5, 2 and 8.
  2. Split into two halves: [9 1 7 6] and [3 5 2 8].
  3. Split again into pairs: [9 1] and [7 6], then [3 5] and [2 8].
  4. Split to single items: 9, 1, 7, 6, 3, 5, 2 and 8, each on its own.
  5. Merge into sorted pairs: [1 9] and [6 7], then [3 5] and [2 8].
  6. Merge pairs into sorted fours: [1 6 7 9] and [2 3 5 8].
  7. Merge into the final list: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for: splitting into two fours, splitting into pairs, splitting into single items, merging into sorted pairs, and merging into sorted fours. The final line is given.
