Idea. Some ciphers swap letters for other letters. The Rail Fence cipher does something different. It keeps the same letters but mixes up their order. That is called a transposition cipher. Picture a fence with a few rails. You write the message in a zig-zag, down to the bottom rail and back up, one letter per column. Here is FENCE on three rails. Then read each rail from left to right, top rail first. Rail one gives F E, rail two gives E C, and rail three gives N. So FENCE becomes F E E C N.

Method. Draw as many rails as the key. Write the letters in a zig-zag, one per column. Read each rail left to right, from top to bottom. Then check the letter count matches. The cipher is weak because there are only a few sensible keys. An attacker can simply try each number of rails until the message makes sense.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper 2 in November 2020. Encrypt the word COMPUTATIONAL using a Rail Fence cipher with a key of four. Show your working. It is worth four marks. Draw four rails. C goes on rail one, O on rail two, M on rail three and P on rail four. Then bounce back up: U on rail three, T on rail two, A on rail one. Keep going. T on rail two, I on rail three, O on rail four, then N on rail three, A on rail two, and L on rail one. Now read along the rails. Rail one is C A L. Rail two is O T T A. Rail three is M U I N. Rail four is P O. Join the rails in order. The ciphertext is C A L O T T A M U I N P O. It has thirteen letters, the same as the plaintext. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is bouncing twice, writing two letters on the bottom rail in a row. Another is reading down the columns, which just gives back the plaintext.

Your turn. Now you try. Encrypt MEET AT NOON, written without spaces, using a Rail Fence cipher with three rails. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Write the zig-zag on three rails. Rail one is M A O. Rail two is E T T O N. Rail three is E N. The ciphertext is M A O E T T O N E N, ten letters, the same as the plaintext. Remember the method: draw the rails, write a zig-zag with one letter per column, read each rail from the top, and check the count. Now try the practice questions for this type.
