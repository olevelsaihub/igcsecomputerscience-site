Read the narration

In denary, if you move every digit one place left and put a zero on the end, forty-five becomes four hundred and fifty. That is ten times bigger. Binary works the same way, but each place left doubles the number. Here is a logical shift left by one place. Zero zero zero one, zero one one zero is twenty-two. Every bit moves one place left, and a zero fills the gap on the right. Now it is forty-four. A logical shift right moves every bit the other way, and a zero fills the gap on the left. Twenty-two shifted right by one becomes eleven. Each place right halves the number, and any remainder is lost. An arithmetic shift keeps the sign of the number. On a right shift, the empty places on the left are filled with copies of the sign bit, the left-most bit. So a negative number stays negative.

First, note three things: the direction, the number of places, and whether the shift is logical or arithmetic. Then move every bit, dropping the bits that fall off the end. Next, fill the gaps. Use zeros for logical shifts and for left shifts. Copy the original sign bit for an arithmetic right shift. Finally, count the bits. The answer must be the same length.

Here is a real question from Paper 1 in June 2023. Apply an arithmetic shift right by three places to one one zero zero, zero one zero one. It is worth one mark. Look at the sign bit, the left-most bit. It is one, so this is a negative number in two's complement. Move every bit three places right. The last three bits, one zero one, fall off the end. One one zero zero zero now sits in the right-hand five places, leaving three gaps on the left. Fill the three gaps with copies of the sign bit, which is one. The answer is one one one one, one zero zero zero. It is still negative, minus eight. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

The most common mistake is filling with zeros after an arithmetic right shift of a negative number. That turns minus fifty-nine into a positive number, which is wrong.

Now you try. Apply a logical shift right by two places to zero one one zero, one one zero zero. Then say what the shift did to the denary value. Pause here and work it out.

Move every bit two places right. The last two bits, zero zero, fall off. Fill the two gaps on the left with zeros. The answer is zero zero zero one, one zero one one. One hundred and eight became twenty-seven, so it was divided by four. Remember the method: read the direction, places and kind; move the bits and drop what falls off; fill with zeros, or the sign bit for arithmetic right; then count the bits.
