Read the narration

Imagine your school puts cameras in every corridor that recognise your face. It might keep you safe. But who sees the video? Did anyone ask you? Questions like these are about ethics: what is right and fair when we use technology. There are three areas to know. Inclusion, privacy and professionalism. Inclusion means everyone can use technology. Barriers include cost, no internet or device at home, a lack of skills, and disability. Some people do not trust online services, or would rather talk to a real person. Privacy is a person's right to control information about themselves. People being watched should give consent. Data protection laws say personal data must be kept secure, used only for stated purposes, and not kept too long. Computing professionals behave legally and ethically. They follow a code of conduct, test their code properly, keep their skills up to date, respect privacy, avoid bias, and make software accessible.

Decide the area. Make each point specific to computing and to the scenario. Give different reasons, never the same one twice. For a privacy discuss, cover consent, storage, use and bias, with a reason for each.

Here is a real question from Paper 1 in June 2024. A research site plans cameras with facial recognition to monitor people. Discuss the privacy issues. It is worth six marks. Plan four privacy ideas: consent, secure storage, how the data is used, and bias. Each one becomes a short paragraph. Faces are personal data, so staff and visitors should be told and asked for consent. The video must be stored securely, encrypted with limited access, and deleted when no longer needed. The data could be misused, such as tracking breaks or who people talk to, so a clear policy is needed. Facial recognition can make more mistakes for some groups, so people could be wrongly flagged. Finally, weigh it up. The site has real security reasons, but it should install the system only if consent, security, limited use and fairness are all handled. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is giving a generic job duty as professionalism. Writing code is just the job. Make it about acting properly. Another mistake is listing words in a discuss answer. Consent, security, bias, with no reasons, earns very little. Develop each point.

Now you try. A town library plans to close its counter and offer book loans only online. Give two reasons why some people might not be able to use the new service. Pause here and work it out.

This is inclusion. Some people cannot afford a device or internet at home. Others lack the skills to use the website, or would rather speak to a real person. Remember the method: name the area, make each point specific, and give a reason for each. Now try the practice questions for this type.
