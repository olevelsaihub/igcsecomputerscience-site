Read the narration

Think of a guessing game: I am thinking of a number from one to a hundred. A smart guesser starts at fifty. If I say lower, half the numbers are gone in one go. Each guess halves what is left. A binary search works the same way. It looks at the middle item of the list first. Each comparison throws away the half that cannot hold the target. It only works if the list is already sorted. In this lesson the positions, called indexes, start at zero. Low is the first index and high is the last. The middle index is low plus high, then DIV two. DIV keeps only the whole number. So zero plus nine, DIV two, is four, not four and a half.

Look at the item at mid. If the target is smaller, throw away the top half: high becomes mid minus one. If it is bigger, throw away the bottom half: low becomes mid plus one. If it matches, you have found it. Write high, low and mid on a new row for each pass, then repeat. Stop when the item is found, or when low becomes bigger than high. That means the item is not in the list.

Here is a real question from Paper 2 in June 2024. The sorted list holds ten numbers, from ten to twenty. A binary search checks whether eleven is in the list. You complete high, low and midpoint for each pass. It is worth four marks. Pass one. Low is zero and high is nine. Mid is zero plus nine, DIV two, which is four. The item at index four is fifteen. Eleven is smaller than fifteen, so the top half goes. High becomes three. Pass two. High is three and low is zero. Mid is zero plus three, DIV two. One and a half is cut down to one. The item at index one is twelve. Eleven is smaller, so high becomes zero. Pass three. High and low are both zero, so mid is zero. The item is ten. Eleven is bigger, so low becomes one. Now low, which is one, is bigger than high, which is zero. The search stops, and eleven is not in the list. The rows are nine, zero, four; then three, zero, one; then zero, zero, zero. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is rounding the midpoint up. DIV always cuts off the fraction, so one and a half becomes one, not two. Another mistake is setting high to mid. The item at mid has already been checked, so high must be mid minus one.

Now you try. The sorted list three, eight, fifteen, twenty-one, twenty-seven, thirty-three, forty has indexes zero to six. Use a binary search to find twenty-seven. Give low, high and mid for each pass. Pause here and work it out.

Pass one: low zero, high six, so mid is three. The item is twenty-one. Twenty-seven is bigger, so low becomes four. Pass two: low four, high six, so mid is five. The item is thirty-three. Twenty-seven is smaller, so high becomes four. Pass three: low and high are both four, so mid is four. The item is twenty-seven. Found, after three items were examined. Remember the method: find mid with DIV, compare, move low or high past mid, and stop when found or when low passes high. Now try the practice questions for this type.
