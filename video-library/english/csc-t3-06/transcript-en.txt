Idea. You already add in columns. Fifty-eight plus twenty-seven: eight plus seven is fifteen, so write five and carry one. Five plus two plus the carry is eight. Eighty-five. Binary has only four rules. Zero plus zero is zero. Zero plus one is one. One plus one is two, written one zero: write zero, carry one. One plus one plus one is three, written one one: write one, carry one. A register is a small store in the processor with a fixed number of bits, often eight. Two hundred and fifty-five plus one needs a ninth bit. When a result is too big for the bits available, that is called overflow.

Method. Line up the two numbers and start at the rightmost column. Add each column with the four rules, writing any carry above the next column. Keep going to the leftmost column. A one carried out of that column means overflow. To explain overflow, name it and give the reason: the result needs more bits than the register has. Finally, check by converting everything to denary.

Worked example. Here is a real question from Paper 1 in June 2022. Add these two eight-bit binary integers: zero zero one one, zero one zero zero, and zero zero zero one, zero one one zero. It is worth two marks. Column one, far right: zero plus zero is zero. Column two: zero plus one is one. Column three: one plus one is one zero. Write zero, carry one. Column four: zero plus zero plus the carry is one. Column five: one plus one is one zero. Write zero, carry one. Column six: one plus zero plus the carry is one zero again. Write zero, carry one. Column seven: zero plus zero plus the carry is one. Column eight: zero plus zero is zero. Nothing is carried out, so there is no overflow. The answer is zero one zero zero, one zero one zero. Here is the whole sum with its carries. Check in denary: fifty-two plus twenty-two is seventy-four, and sixty-four plus eight plus two is seventy-four. Each correct nibble earns a mark. Common mistake. A common mistake is writing two in a column. Binary has no digit two. One plus one is one zero, so write zero and carry one. Another mistake is saying only that there was an error. Name it as overflow, and say why: the result needs more bits than are available.

Answer. Right nibble. Zero plus one is one. One plus zero is one. One plus one is one zero: write zero, carry one. Zero plus zero plus the carry is one. That gives one zero one one. Left nibble. One plus zero is one. One plus one is one zero: write zero, carry one. Zero plus one plus the carry is one zero: write zero, carry one. One plus zero plus the carry is one zero: write zero, and carry one out of the left. That carry out means overflow. The result needs nine bits, but only eight are available, so the ninth bit is lost. Check: one hundred and eighty-two plus one hundred and one is two hundred and eighty-three, more than two hundred and fifty-five. Remember the method: add right to left with the four rules, write every carry, and watch the leftmost column. Now try the practice questions for this type.
