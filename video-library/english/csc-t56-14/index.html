Read the narration

Your phone did not appear from nowhere. Metals were mined, a factory used energy and water to build it, it uses electricity every day, and one day it will be thrown away. Each stage affects the environment. Making devices uses large amounts of energy and water, and causes air and water pollution. Mining metals such as copper, gold and lithium uses up limited resources and can poison soil and groundwater. Running computers and data centres uses a lot of electricity, including for cooling. Thrown-away electronics, called e-waste, contain toxic materials that can leak from landfill into the ground and water. We can reduce the impact. Switch devices off, lower screen brightness, keep devices longer, repair them, recycle them properly, or donate them. Keeping a device longer means fewer new ones are made. Technology can also help health. Smartwatches measure heart rate, steps, sleep and blood oxygen, and encourage exercise. They can share data with doctors and send alerts. But they only help if the user acts on the information.

Decide the stage: making, using, throwing away, or health. Give a specific impact or action. For explain, add so, then the benefit. For a six-mark discuss, cover several points, develop each one, and add balance.

Here is a real question from Paper 1 in June 2025. Discuss how the use of wearable technology, such as smartwatches, can be a benefit to health. It is worth six marks. Sensors measure heart rate, blood oxygen and sleep. This gives a clear daily picture, so people can notice problems early and get help. G P S and motion sensors record steps and distance. Athletes use this to plan training and track progress, so they improve without overtraining. The watch sends data to a phone app, which can share trends with a coach or doctor. It can remind you to move, or alert family if an elderly person falls. Now balance it. Goals and badges motivate many people, but some stop wearing the device or ignore its advice. So wearables help health only if the wearer acts on the information. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is a vague action, like be eco-friendly or use it less. Give a specific action, then say what it achieves.

Now you try. A school replaces two hundred laptops every two years. Give two actions, other than using less energy, to reduce the environmental impact. Explain one of them. Pause here and work it out.

First, keep the laptops longer and repair them. Second, recycle or donate the old ones. Keeping them longer means fewer new laptops are made, so less mining, less manufacturing and less e-waste. Remember the method: pick the stage, give a specific action, then say so, and the benefit. Now try the practice questions for this type.
