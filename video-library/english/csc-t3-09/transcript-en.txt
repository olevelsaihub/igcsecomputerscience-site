Standard ASCII uses seven bits for each character. Two to the power seven is one hundred and twenty-eight, so there are one hundred and twenty-eight codes, from nought to one hundred and twenty-seven. The letters run in order. Here is a real question from Paper 1 in June 2022. Explain one reason to use Unicode instead of ASCII for languages other than English. It is worth two marks. The point: languages such as Arabic, Chinese or Urdu use many characters that are not English letters. ASCII has only seven bits, which is one hundred and twenty-eight codes. That is enough for English, but not for them. The reason: Unicode uses sixteen or more bits, which gives sixty-five thousand five hundred and thirty-six codes or more. A common mistake is saying standard ASCII uses eight bits, with two hundred and fifty-six characters. That is extended ASCII. Standard ASCII is seven bits. Another mistake is counting one too many. H is seven letters after A, not eight. Count the steps between, not the letters.

Small e is four steps after small a, so ninety-seven plus four is one hundred and one. One hundred and one is sixty-four plus thirty-two plus four plus one. The answer is one one zero zero one zero one.
