Imagine swapping every letter of a secret note for a little shape. Only a friend with the same key can swap them back. That is a substitution cipher. Here is the word HELLO written in the Pigpen cipher. The key is a set of grids. The first is a noughts-and-crosses grid holding A to I. A letter's symbol is made of the lines around its cell. E sits in the centre, so its symbol is a full square. The second grid is the same shape, holding J to R, but every symbol from it has a dot inside. J is top left, so its symbol is two lines, right and bottom, with a dot. The last two parts are X shapes. S to V sit in a plain X, and W to Z in a dotted X: top, left, right and bottom. T is in the left triangle, so its two lines make an arrowhead pointing right.

To encrypt a letter, find it in the key. Note which grid it is in, and its position. Draw only the lines that border its cell. Add a dot if it is in a dotted grid. To decrypt, work backwards. Match the outline and the dot to a cell, and read its letter. To judge a key, check every letter has a symbol, and no symbol is used twice.

Here is a real question from Paper 2 in June 2022. Figure one shows a Pigpen key. Give the symbol for each letter in the word MAY. It is worth three marks. M is in the dotted grid, middle row, left cell. It has a line above, a line below and a line on its right. So its symbol is a box open on the left, with a dot inside. A is in the plain grid, top-left cell. It has a line on its right and a line below. So its symbol is a corner made of those two lines, with no dot. Y is in the dotted X, right-hand triangle. Its two lines meet at the centre, on its left, like an arrowhead pointing left, with a dot inside. So the word MAY is written with these three symbols. Each correct symbol earns one mark. A common mistake is forgetting the dot. Look at these two symbols. The first is D, from the plain grid. The second is M. The only difference is the dot.

Now you try. Using the same key, draw the Pigpen symbols for the word TREE. Pause here and work it out.

T is in the plain X, left triangle: an arrowhead pointing right, no dot. R is in the dotted grid, bottom right: lines on its top and left, with a dot. E is the centre of the plain grid: a full square, drawn twice. Remember the method: find the letter's grid and cell, draw only the lines around that cell, and add a dot for the dotted grids.
