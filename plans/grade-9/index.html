Cloud storage: what it is, pros, cons and security

Answer plan · June 2023 · Paper 1 · Q6(d)
  1. Write one sentence defining cloud storage; optionally one on why it has grown.
  2. Benefit 1: access from anywhere and sharing or collaboration with permissions; explain how it helps the programmers.
  3. Benefit 2: lower cost and scalable space, plus automatic backups; explain what problem each removes.
  4. Drawback 1: needs a reliable internet connection and depends on the provider staying available.
  5. Drawback 2: security, privacy and legal control of data held by another company; mention how encryption reduces the risk.
  6. Conclude with a judgement (do benefits outweigh drawbacks?) and the conditions that make it true.

Translators: assembler, compiler and interpreter

Answer plan · November 2020 · Paper 1 · Q6(c)
  1. Note the command word: discuss similarities AND differences of compiler and interpreter.
  2. Open with 2 similarities: both turn high-level code into machine code; one statement becomes many instructions; both find errors.
  3. Difference 1: whole program at once and saved executable vs one line at a time, nothing saved; explain the effect on run speed.
  4. Difference 2: error reporting; list at the end vs stops at the first error; explain why interpreters help while testing.
  5. Difference 3: distribution; executable needs no extra software and hides source but is machine-specific vs interpreter needed on every machine, source visible but portable.
  6. Write each difference as a pair joined by 'whereas' or 'but', then say what it means for the user or programmer.
  7. Conclude: which suits development and which suits the finished product, linked to the scenario.

Finding and fixing weaknesses: penetration testing, ethical hacking, code reviews, audit trails

Answer plan · June 2022 · Paper 1 · Q6(d)
  1. List the three methods named in the question: ethical hacking, commercial analysis tools, review of policies.
  2. Ethical hacking: who does it, what they try (with permission), what Santiago receives (a report of weaknesses), one drawback.
  3. Analysis tools: what a scanner checks automatically, the report it produces, why running it regularly helps, one limitation.
  4. Policy review: what the policies are (passwords, access rights, devices), why they go out of date, why reviews must be scheduled.
  5. Keep linking each method to finding AND fixing weaknesses in Santiago's network.
  6. Conclude: each method covers a different type of weakness, so use them together.

Protecting software by law: copyright, patents, trademarks and licences

Answer plan · November 2021 · Paper 1 · Q6(d)
  1. Note the two bullet points you must use: intellectual property and licensing; keep using Akiko's name.
  2. Intellectual property: define it, say copyright is automatic, explain what it lets her do (stop copying, take legal action); mention patent or trademark briefly.
  3. Licensing: buying software means buying a licence; describe a proprietary licence and what it forbids.
  4. Technical enforcement: licence keys, online registration or activation, supplying compiled code only; explain how each stops illegal copies.
  5. Balance: open-source or Creative Commons if she wants to share, with conditions she controls.
  6. Conclude: combine legal protection with the right licence and activation checks.

Ethics of technology: privacy, inclusion and professionalism

Answer plan · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Q4(f)
  1. Note the focus: privacy issues in deciding whether to install cameras with facial recognition; mention Peter, Marie or the site.
  2. Point 1: faces are personal (biometric) data; data protection rules, telling people, consent.
  3. Point 2: proportionality; monitoring restricted areas vs tracking every movement; effect on trust.
  4. Point 3: how the data is used and who is accountable; risk of using it for other purposes; retention and deletion.
  5. Point 4: keeping the data secure from hackers; possible bias or misidentification.
  6. Show both sides: the security benefit the facility wants vs the privacy cost to staff.
  7. Conclude: install only if limited, transparent, secure and legal.

Technology, the environment and health

Answer plan · June 2021 · Paper 1 · Q6(c)
  1. Spot the two things asked for: negative environmental impacts AND actions to reduce them.
  2. Organise impacts by life cycle: making (mining, water, energy, pollution), using (electricity, cooling), disposing (toxic e-waste, landfill).
  3. For each impact, explain why it harms the environment, not just name it.
  4. Give 3-4 actions and link each one to the impact it tackles (switch off saves energy; recycling reduces mining; donating extends life).
  5. Mention who can act: individuals, manufacturers or sellers, governments.
  6. Conclude: the impact cannot be removed but can be reduced, saying which actions matter most.

  1. Note the focus: benefits to health of wearables, and the groups named (athletes and others).

Emerging tech: quantum, DNA computing, AI and nanotechnology

Answer plan · June 2019 · Paper 1 · Q6(c)
  1. Underline the three areas you must cover: characteristics, uses, ethical issues.
  2. Characteristics (1 short paragraph): trained on example data, finds patterns, predicts for new data, can improve over time.
  3. Uses (2 developed points): pick familiar areas such as recommendations, face or speech recognition, medical diagnosis; for each say what the AI does and why it helps.
  4. Ethical issues (2-3 developed points): biased training data, decisions nobody can explain or check, privacy of collected data, job losses; link each back to how AI learns.
  5. Use connectives (because, so, this means, however) so every point leads to a consequence.
  6. Conclude: agree or disagree with the statement and name what would make the impact positive (checked data, human oversight, laws).

The final 20-mark program

Answer plan · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Q5(whole)
  1. Read the task bullets and tick each one off as an output you must print: total, average, IDs under ten, three medal winners with names.
  2. Look at the record structure in Q05 (which index is ID, first name, last name, books) and write it as a comment.
  3. Initialise total and counter variables before any loop.
  4. Write one loop over the whole list (never a fixed 20) that adds books, counts pupils and prints IDs under ten.
  5. After the loop, print the total and the average (total divided by count).
  6. Find gold, silver and bronze, by sorting a copy or by tracking the top three, and print ID and full name for each.
  7. Add short comments above each stage and use meaningful variable names.
  8. Test: change one pupil's book count or delete a record and check the outputs still make sense; save as Q05FINISHED.py.
